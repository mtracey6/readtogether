Why it exists

Built from a classroom constraint—not a trend brief.

8 yearsof K–5 planning, differentiation, assessment, intervention, and family communication behind the product judgment.
01

Keep the content rigorous.

Learners can work toward the same grade-level idea while receiving different amounts of language support. The prototype changes the scaffold—not the intellectual goal.

02

Give teachers structure, not a wall of text.

The output separates content and language objectives, vocabulary, sentence frames, formative checks, and a review prompt so each part can be inspected and edited.

03

Make the safety boundary visible.

The proposed model request is assembled from a narrow allow-list of instructional parameters. Names, rosters, audio, IEP information, service status, and open-ended learner notes are outside it.

04

Say exactly what the evidence supports.

This project demonstrates product framing, instructional design, interaction design, and full-stack prototyping. It does not demonstrate usability, adoption, or learning impact because no participant research has been conducted.

Product + technical judgment

The interesting work is around the model call.

The broader codebase pairs a React client with a Bun/Hono API and a Prisma/SQLite data layer. The portfolio build keeps the model offline and exposes the boundary instead.

Structured before generated

A typed output shape makes lesson parts editable, reusable, and testable. The teacher receives a draft with visible components—not an opaque paragraph.

Evidence boundary

A designed prototype, presented as one.

No student, educator, family, school, or district has evaluated ReadTogether. That limits what I can claim—and clarifies what should happen next.

What exists today

  • Public, interactive lesson-scaffold demonstration
  • Designed lesson-output structure and teacher review pattern
  • Full-stack prototype architecture and source code
  • Explicit model-data boundary and risk assumptions

What has not happened

  • Participant interviews or usability sessions
  • Classroom implementation or district review
  • Adoption, time-saved, or learning-impact measurement
  • Security, accessibility, or instructional-quality validation
Is it useful?

Is it instructionally sound?

Is the boundary real?

Test authorization and confirm learner information cannot enter model requests.
